# Code of Conduct

Young AI Leaders is a community, and this assignment is part of how people join it. We
want everyone taking part — candidates, reviewers, and the Software & Research team — to
have a straightforward and respectful experience.

## What we expect

- **Treat people with respect.** Disagree with ideas, not with people.
- **Be honest about your work.** Say what you did, what you did not do, and what you are
  unsure about. Report your findings as you actually found them.
- **Credit others.** If an idea, method, or line of code came from a tutorial, a colleague,
  or an AI assistant, say so. Attribution costs nothing and is never held against you.
- **Respect confidentiality.** The survey data belongs to the people who answered it.
  Handle it as described in [DATA_USE.md](DATA_USE.md).
- **Ask when something is unclear.** A question is not a weakness. We would rather answer
  it than review work built on a misunderstanding.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, discrimination, or demeaning comments — including on the basis of age,
  gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, ethnicity,
  nationality, religion, or level of experience
- Personal attacks, sustained hostility, or deliberate intimidation
- Publishing anyone's private information without their permission
- Misrepresenting your work: presenting someone else's analysis as your own, or
  fabricating or altering results
- Breaching the confidentiality of the dataset, or of another candidate's work

## Scope

This applies to everything connected with the assignment and this repository: emails,
submissions, interviews, and any YAIL space where the work is discussed.

## Reporting a concern

If you experience or witness behaviour that falls short of this, contact
**office@youngaileaderslinz.at**. Reports are read by the Software & Research team and
handled discreetly. Tell us what happened and what you would like to see done; we will not
take action beyond what you are comfortable with unless someone's safety requires it.

We take reports seriously and we do not penalise people for making them in good faith.

## Consequences

Depending on what happened, we may ask someone to change their behaviour, decline to
continue with an application, or end someone's participation in the community. Breaches of
confidentiality or of research integrity are the most serious and are handled with the
study's academic supervisors.
